During this course, you will use your laptop to connect to Draco, the high-performance computing (HPC) cluster at the University of Jena. Most analyses will run on Draco rather than on your laptop.

> ## Setup checkpoint.
>
> If you completed the [pre-course setup](https://mgxlab.github.io/Viromics2026/laptop_setup/index.html), you should already be able to connect to Draco from VS Code.
> 1. Open VS Code
> 2. Connect to Draco using Remote - SSH (as you did before the course).
> 3. Open a terminal and run:
>
>  ```bash
> hostname
> pwd
> whoami
> ```
> `hostname` should print something like login1.cluster or login2.cluster.
> 
> `pwd` should print your home directory on Draco, something like /home/<your_username>.
> 
> `whoami` should print your URZ username.
>
> **If anything doesn't work, flag a TA now — it's much faster to fix this before we rely on it all day.**

## Why Draco at all?

During this course you will work with sequencing datasets that are too large, and analyses that are too slow, to run comfortably on a laptop. Draco is the University of Jena's shared high-performance computing (HPC) cluster. We'll unpack exactly how it's organised, and why that matters for how you run programs, a bit later today — for now, the key idea is simply: your laptop and Draco are two different computers, and VS Code is just the window you use to see and control both.
